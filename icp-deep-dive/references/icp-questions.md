# ICP Interview Questions

Use these questions to build each persona card. Cover all six dimensions for every persona. Cowork's wizard UI will handle pacing automatically — just present questions in priority order.

For each question, accept the user's answer at face value and don't push back unless the answer is clearly too generic. If they say "Gen Z women," ask one follow-up: "Pick one actual customer you talked to recently — what was she like?"

---

## Dimension 1 — Identity (3-4 questions)

1. **Age range**
   What's the age range of this persona? Pick the tightest range that captures most of them. (e.g., 28-38)

2. **Job/role/life stage**
   What do they do for work? What's their life stage? (e.g., early-career professional, new parent, empty-nester, student)

3. **Family situation**
   Are they single? Married? Kids? This affects buying psychology more than most brands realize.

4. **Geographic context**
   Urban, suburban, or rural? Any region that's overrepresented? (Skip if location doesn't matter for the brand.)

---

## Dimension 2 — Lifestyle and Values (3-4 questions)

1. **How they spend their time**
   What does a typical week look like for this persona? Work, hobbies, family time, content consumption.

2. **What they care about**
   Pick 3-5 things this persona genuinely values. Health? Productivity? Status? Sustainability? Family? Adventure?

3. **What they want more of**
   What's missing from their life that they wish they had more of? (Time, energy, confidence, recognition, freedom.)

4. **What they're trying to escape**
   What are they actively trying to avoid or get away from? (Burnout, boredom, financial stress, feeling out of shape.)

---

## Dimension 3 — The Specific Pain (4-5 questions)

This is the most important dimension. Push for specificity.

1. **The exact problem**
   What's the specific problem this persona has that your brand solves? Not "they want to feel better" — what's actually broken in their life right now?

2. **How long they've had it**
   Has this been a problem for weeks, months, years? Acute vs. chronic matters for messaging urgency.

3. **What they've tried**
   List the things this persona has already tried that didn't work. Competitor products, free alternatives, lifestyle changes, etc.

4. **What it's costing them**
   What does the problem cost this persona? Time, money, energy, confidence, relationships, opportunities? Be specific.

5. **The emotional cost**
   How does the problem make them FEEL? Frustrated? Embarrassed? Resigned? Overwhelmed? This shapes the empathetic angle in copy.

---

## Dimension 4 — Buying Triggers (3-4 questions)

1. **The "I need this NOW" moment**
   Describe the specific moment this persona decides they're ready to buy. What just happened that pushed them over the edge?

2. **Internal narrative**
   What's the self-talk in their head when they're about to buy? "I deserve this," "I can't keep doing this," "Everyone else seems to have figured this out," etc.

3. **External trigger**
   Is there an event, deadline, season, or social pressure that drives the purchase? (Wedding, summer, new job, pregnancy, holiday, breakup.)

4. **Budget psychology**
   How does this persona feel about price? Price-sensitive (looking for the cheapest option), value-driven (will pay more if convinced of ROI), or premium-comfortable (assumes higher price = better quality)?

---

## Dimension 5 — Objections and Hesitations (3-4 questions)

1. **Top hesitations**
   What are the top 2-3 reasons this persona might NOT buy from your brand even after they're aware of it?

2. **What they're skeptical about**
   What claims feel too good to be true? What sounds like marketing BS to them? What category-wide trust issues do they have?

3. **What needs to be proven**
   What evidence does this persona need to see before they trust your brand? Reviews? Clinical data? Founder story? Money-back guarantee? Free trial?

4. **Competitors they're comparing**
   When this persona is considering your brand, who else are they evaluating? Direct competitors, indirect alternatives, or "do nothing"?

---

## Dimension 6 — Language and Media (4-5 questions)

This dimension is gold for copy skills. Push for real, specific phrases — not generic descriptions.

1. **Voice-of-customer phrases**
   What are the exact words this persona uses to describe their problem? If you have customer reviews, pull them. If not, ask: "If you could record this person venting to a friend about the problem, what would they say?"

2. **Where they spend time online**
   Which platforms? (Instagram, TikTok, Reddit, YouTube, podcasts, newsletters, niche forums.)

3. **Who they follow**
   Specific creators, influencers, or accounts that this persona engages with. This reveals aspirational identity and content style preferences.

4. **What they read/watch/listen to**
   Publications, shows, podcasts, newsletters they consume regularly. This reveals their information diet and attention patterns.

5. **What ads/brands they respond to**
   Examples of marketing this persona has responded to in the past — for your brand or others. This reveals their persuasion patterns.

---

## Bonus Question (if user has time)

**The "movie scene" question:**
> Imagine a 30-second movie scene that opens an ad targeting this persona. What does the scene look like? Where are they? What are they doing? What's the emotion on their face?

This is optional but it forces specificity. The answer often becomes the seed for ad scripts later.

---

## Question Ordering Strategy

For most personas, ask in this order:

1. Identity (quick, easy, low-friction warmup)
2. The Specific Pain (the most important dimension — get this right)
3. Lifestyle and Values (broader context after the pain is anchored)
4. Buying Triggers (now that we know the pain, when do they act?)
5. Objections (after the buying scenario, what stops them?)
6. Language and Media (final dimension — capture real phrases and channels)

Skip the bonus question unless the user is engaged and wants to go deeper.

---

## When to Push Back vs. Accept the Answer

**Accept and move on:**
- Specific demographic answers
- Concrete pain descriptions
- Real quoted customer language
- Named competitors or platforms

**Push back gently and ask for specificity:**
- "Women 25-45" (too broad — ask for one real customer)
- "They want to feel better" (too vague — ask what specifically is broken)
- "They like our product" (not an answer — ask what almost stopped them from buying)
- "Everyone" (no — narrow to one persona)

The goal is to surface real, usable insights that downstream copy skills can act on. Vague answers produce vague copy. Specific answers produce specific copy that converts.
